# Product discovery skill

1. Translate the idea into testable hypotheses, not assumed truths.
2. Search current public discussions, release notes, competitor docs/listings, and official product docs. Prefer direct user statements and primary sources.
3. Keep an evidence ledger: source URL, posted/published date, retrieval date, user segment, need, workaround, strength, and caveats.
4. Separate direct evidence, competitor marketing, interpretation, and unknowns. Never invent ratings, review counts, install counts, willingness to pay, or interviews.
5. Cluster repeated pain into jobs-to-be-done. Describe the current workaround and why it is painful; “users want feature X” is not enough.
6. Audit existing substitutes and reasons not to switch. Do not select a wedge that competitors already solve equally well without a verified difference.
7. Recommend one primary segment, one primary job, a minimal value proposition, top risks, and falsification tests. State confidence explicitly.
8. Keep broad features out of MVP unless evidence shows they are required for the primary job.
9. End with a short user-validation plan and concrete activation/retention metrics. Research is not proof of product-market fit.
